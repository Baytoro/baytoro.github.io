Better & Faster Large Language Models via Multi-Token Prediction

Gloeckle F, Idrissi B Y, Rozière B, Lopez-Paz D, Synnaeve G. Better & Faster Large Language Models via Multi-token Prediction.

Paper

Multi-token prediction changes the language-modeling objective. Instead of predicting only the next token at each position, the model predicts several future tokens using multiple output heads on top of a shared trunk.

Objective

At position (t), the model predicts (n) future tokens:

The first head is the ordinary next-token head. Additional heads predict farther future positions.

The figure shows the 4-token prediction setup and MBPP gains. The gains become more visible at larger model sizes.

Why It Helps

Next-token prediction gives one supervised target per position. Multi-token prediction gives denser future-token supervision. This can improve sample efficiency, especially for tasks that require planning or algorithmic structure.

The method is also attractive because the trunk is shared. Training time does not grow like training multiple independent models.

Inference Use

At inference time, the model can ignore the extra heads and use only next-token prediction. Alternatively, the extra heads can serve as draft heads for self-speculative decoding.

This connects the paper to Medusa and DeepSeek-V3’s MTP module. The difference is that multi-token prediction is part of the pretraining objective, not only a post-hoc acceleration head.

Risks

The loss weights (\lambda_k) matter. If far-future heads dominate the objective, they may interfere with next-token modeling. Farther tokens are also more uncertain, so more heads are not automatically better.

Takeaway

Multi-token prediction is a clean objective-level modification. It improves training signal density and creates a natural path to draft-token generation without maintaining a separate draft model.
