TernaryBERT: Distillation-aware Ultra-low Bit BERT

H.J. Chen
  2020-12-23 2020-12-23 Created

Paper: TernaryBERT: Distillation-aware Ultra-low Bit BERT

TernaryBERT is a training recipe for an ultra-low-bit BERT student. The core setting in the paper is written as W-E-A, meaning the number of bits used for Transformer weights, word embeddings, and activations. The interesting configuration is 2-2-8: Transformer weights are ternary, word embeddings are ternary, and activations entering linear or matrix-multiplication operators are quantized to 8 bits.

This is not post-training quantization. At 2-bit weight precision, the student no longer has enough capacity to recover task behavior only from labels. The paper therefore treats quantization and distillation as one package:

  1. keep full-precision latent weights during training;
  2. ternarize weights for the forward pass;
  3. quantize selected activations to 8 bits;
  4. train the quantized student with Transformer-layer and prediction-layer distillation from a full-precision teacher.

Why This Paper Matters

The paper is trying to occupy a difficult point on the size-accuracy curve: much smaller than 8-bit BERT and dense distillation-only models, but still close to full-precision BERT on GLUE and SQuAD.

Figure 1 from the paper. Red squares are the authors’ methods on MNLI-m. The key point is that TernaryBERT reaches Q-BERT-level or better accuracy with smaller model size.

The most important number is not only the 2-bit weight format. It is the resulting model size. BERT-base is 418 MB in the paper’s accounting. TernaryBERT at 2-2-8 is 28 MB, or 14.9x smaller. TernaryTinyBERT at 2-2-8 is 18 MB, or 23.2x smaller.

The paper also includes a 3-bit extension, but I read the 2-bit result as the main contribution. At 8 bits, BERT quantization is already relatively forgiving. At 2 bits, the training recipe becomes the method.

What Is Quantized

The paper quantizes the dominant dense parts of BERT:

  • attention projection weights W_Q, W_K, W_V, and W_O;
  • feed-forward weights W_1 and W_2;
  • word embedding weights;
  • inputs to linear layers and matrix multiplication operations.

It does not quantize everything. Segment embeddings, position embeddings, linear biases, Softmax, LayerNorm, and the final task-specific layer are kept out of the low-bit path. The authors argue that these parameters are small, and quantizing the numerically sensitive operators can cause noticeable accuracy loss.

This scope matters. TernaryBERT is aggressive about BERT’s large matrices, but it is not an integer-only Transformer in the I-BERT sense.

Granularity

TernaryBERT tests layer-wise and row-wise scaling. The final empirical choice is:

  • row-wise ternarization for word embeddings;
  • layer-wise ternarization for Transformer-layer weights.

The reasoning is practical. Word embeddings contain word-specific semantic information, so a separate scale per row helps. Transformer weight matrices are highly redundant, and one scale per matrix is often enough. In the ablation, row-wise embedding plus layer-wise Transformer weights gives the best MNLI pair among the tested TWN settings: 83.3 on MNLI-m and 83.3 on MNLI-mm.

Activation Quantization

Weights are ternary, but activations are 8-bit. The paper quantizes activations that feed linear layers and matrix multiplications. It compares symmetric 8-bit quantization with min-max 8-bit quantization and chooses min-max.

The reason is visible in the hidden-state distributions.

Distillation-Aware Training

The framework is a teacher-student loop. The teacher is a full-precision BERT fine-tuned on the downstream task. The student starts from the same fine-tuned model, but each forward pass uses ternary weights.

Figure 2 from the paper. The quantized student is trained against a full-precision teacher through Transformer-layer and prediction-layer distillation.

The full-precision latent weights are kept during training. At step t, the student weight w_t is ternarized to \hat{w}_t, the quantized model runs the forward pass, the distillation loss is computed, and the gradient is used to update the latent full-precision w_t. GLUE Results

Tables 1 and 2 from the paper. Development and test results on GLUE.

Several rows are worth reading carefully:

  • BERT baseline: 32-32-32, 418 MB, dev MNLI 84.5/84.9.
  • TernaryBERT-TWN: 2-2-8, 28 MB, dev MNLI 83.3/83.3.
  • TernaryBERT-LAT: 2-2-8, 28 MB, dev MNLI 83.5/83.4.
  • Q-BERT: 2-8-8, 43 MB, dev MNLI 76.6/77.0.
  • Q2BERT: 2-8-8, 43 MB, dev MNLI 47.2/47.3.
  • TernaryTinyBERT-TWN: 2-2-8, 18 MB, dev MNLI 83.4/83.8.

The surprising part is not that 2-bit BERT is slightly below fp32 BERT. That is expected. The surprising part is that TernaryBERT stays close while Q-BERT-style 2-bit baselines collapse much harder in the same table.

On the GLUE test set, the full BERT baseline gets a score of 78.2. TernaryBERT-TWN at 2-2-32 gets 77.3, TernaryBERT-TWN at 2-2-8 gets 76.9, and TernaryTinyBERT-TWN gets 76.6. The Tiny version loses 1.6 points while being 23.2x smaller.

SQuAD Results

Table 3 from the paper. Development results on SQuAD v1.1 and v2.0.

SQuAD is a useful stress test because span extraction depends on token-level representations, not just classification logits. The result pattern still holds:

  • BERT: 418 MB, SQuAD v1.1 81.5/88.7, SQuAD v2.0 74.5/77.7.
  • Q-BERT: 43 MB, SQuAD v1.1 69.7/79.6.
  • Q2BERT: 43 MB, SQuAD v2.0 50.1/50.1.
  • TernaryBERT-TWN: 28 MB, SQuAD v1.1 79.9/87.4, SQuAD v2.0 73.1/76.4.
  • TernaryBERT-LAT: 28 MB, SQuAD v1.1 80.1/87.5, SQuAD v2.0 73.3/76.6.

For SQuAD, LAT is slightly better than TWN. The paper also notes that the Transformer-layer loss is much larger than the prediction-layer loss, so SQuAD training is done in two stages: first with L_trm, then with the full loss.

Ablations

The ablations are the most useful part of the paper because they show which pieces are load-bearing.

Table 5 from the paper. Min-max activation quantization beats symmetric activation quantization on SQuAD v1.1.

With 2-2-8, symmetric activation quantization gets 79.0 EM and 86.9 F1 on SQuAD v1.1. Min-max activation quantization gets 79.9 EM and 87.4 F1. This matches the hidden-state distribution observation: the activations are not symmetric enough for the symmetric grid to be ideal.

Table 6 from the paper. Removing Transformer-layer distillation and logit distillation hurts, especially on CoLA, RTE, and SQuAD.

The full TernaryBERT-TWN row gets 55.7 on CoLA, 72.9 on RTE, and 79.9/87.4 on SQuAD v1.1. Without Transformer-layer distillation, those become 52.7, 69.0, and 76.6/84.9. With both Transformer-layer and logit distillation removed, they fall to 45.4, 56.3, and 74.3/83.2.

That is the central lesson: final labels alone are too weak for this bit-width.

Table 7 from the paper. Data augmentation and initialization from a fine-tuned BERT both matter.

Initialization also matters. If the student is initialized from pre-trained BERT instead of the already fine-tuned BERT, CoLA drops from 55.7 to 46.0 and RTE drops from 72.9 to 66.4. Data augmentation also helps, especially on CoLA and RTE.

Compression Comparison

Table 8 from the paper. MNLI-m comparison against quantization and compression baselines.

This table mixes several families of methods, so I read it as a size-accuracy map rather than a pure apples-to-apples benchmark:

  • DistilBERT: 250 MB, 81.6 MNLI-m.
  • TinyBERT-4L: 55 MB, 82.8 MNLI-m.
  • Quant-Noise: 38 MB, 83.6 MNLI-m.
  • Q-BERT 2/4-8-8: 53 MB, 83.5 MNLI-m.
  • 3-bit BERT: 41 MB, 84.2 MNLI-m.
  • 3-bit TinyBERT: 25 MB, 83.7 MNLI-m.
  • TernaryBERT: 28 MB, 83.5 MNLI-m.
  • TernaryTinyBERT: 18 MB, 83.4 MNLI-m.

TernaryBERT is not always the highest-accuracy point. The 3-bit BERT row is higher on MNLI-m. But TernaryBERT and TernaryTinyBERT give a strong size-accuracy tradeoff with a simpler fixed low-bit setting than mixed-precision methods.

Attention Patterns

The appendix checks whether ternarization destroys attention structure.

Figure 6 from the appendix. Attention maps from full-precision BERT and TernaryBERT on a CoLA example remain visually similar.

I would not over-read this figure. Visual attention similarity is not a proof of functional equivalence. But it is a useful diagnostic: the ternary student is not merely matching logits while internally behaving randomly. The layer and attention distillation losses appear to preserve at least some internal structure.

Relation To Q8BERT And BinaryBERT

Q8BERT is the conservative baseline: use quantization-aware fine-tuning to put embedding and fully connected paths into INT8 while leaving numerically sensitive operations in FP32. TernaryBERT is more aggressive on weights and word embeddings, but it still keeps activations at 8 bits and leaves Softmax/LayerNorm out of the quantized path.

BinaryBERT is the more extreme follow-up direction. It asks how to train 1-bit BERT weights, and it uses a ternary model as an intermediate bridge. From that angle, TernaryBERT is the boundary where BERT quantization starts to require serious training machinery, but still has a zero state that makes optimization manageable.

The pattern across these papers is clean:

  • 8-bit BERT can often be handled with QAT.
  • 2-bit ternary BERT needs distillation and careful initialization.
  • 1-bit binary BERT needs even more structure, such as ternary-to-binary splitting.

Reproduction Checklist

If I were reproducing TernaryBERT, I would separate the moving parts:

  • Start from a task-fine-tuned full-precision BERT teacher.
  • Define the exact quantized operator coverage before training. Do not silently quantize Softmax, LayerNorm, or the task head.
  • Keep full-precision latent weights and ternarize only for the forward path.
  • Implement TWN first; add LAT only after the TWN baseline is stable.
  • Use row-wise scaling for word embeddings and layer-wise scaling for Transformer weights.
  • Use min-max 8-bit activation quantization, not only symmetric activation quantization.
  • Add distillation in stages: hidden states, attention scores, then logits.
  • Run the ablations without L_trm, without logit distillation, without data augmentation, and without fine-tuned initialization.
  • Report W-E-A, model size, and task metrics together. A bit-width claim without size accounting is ambiguous.

For deployment, I would also benchmark the actual runtime path. Ternary weights reduce model storage, but speedup depends on whether the inference backend has kernels that exploit ternary weights and 8-bit activations efficiently.

Limitations

The method is task-training heavy. It needs a fine-tuned full-precision teacher, distillation losses, and in some cases data augmentation. This is not the same convenience level as post-training quantization.

The quantized scope is also partial. Activations are 8-bit, not ternary. Softmax, LayerNorm, biases, position/segment embeddings, and the task-specific layer are not quantized. That is a reasonable engineering choice, but it means the method should not be described as a fully ternary BERT inference graph.

Finally, model size is not latency. A 28 MB TernaryBERT is attractive, but practical speed depends on packing, kernels, memory layout, and whether the hardware can turn ternary arithmetic into real throughput gains.

Takeaway

TernaryBERT is best read as a recipe for making 2-bit BERT trainable. The ternary format gives compression, but distillation gives the optimization signal that keeps the student close to the teacher.

The paper’s strongest lesson is that ultra-low-bit Transformer compression is not just a quantizer choice. At 2 bits, initialization, activation quantization, scaling granularity, hidden-state distillation, attention distillation, and logit distillation all matter.
