SNAS: Stochastic Neural Architecture Search

H.J. Chen
  2020-08-28 2020-08-28 Created  
  • Paper Reading 
 
  • NAS 

Xie S, Zheng H, Liu C, Lin L. SNAS: Stochastic Neural Architecture Search. ICLR 2019.

Paper

SNAS is part of the differentiable NAS family, but it is not just DARTS with a different name. DARTS uses a soft mixture of candidate operations. SNAS treats architecture choices as stochastic variables and optimizes a distribution over discrete architectures using the Concrete/Gumbel-Softmax relaxation.

This makes the search-time model closer to sampled discrete architectures instead of a full multi-path mixture.

Stochastic Architecture Variables

For each edge, SNAS samples an operation indicator (z). The optimization objective can be viewed as:

The architecture parameters (\alpha) control the distribution (p_\alpha(z)). Since direct categorical sampling is not differentiable, SNAS uses a differentiable relaxation.

The figure shows the forward path sampling view. The method keeps the discreteness of sampled architectures more explicit than a pure soft mixture.

Gumbel-Softmax

The relaxed sample is controlled by a temperature (\tau). High temperature makes samples soft and gradients stable. Low temperature makes samples closer to one-hot choices but increases gradient variance.

The temperature schedule is therefore not a minor implementation detail. If it cools too quickly, search may collapse early. If it stays too high, the final discrete architecture may not match the search-time model.

Relation To GDAS

SNAS and GDAS are close in spirit. Both use stochastic differentiable sampling. SNAS emphasizes the stochastic objective; GDAS emphasizes efficient one-path sampling during search.

Takeaway

SNAS is important because it shows differentiable NAS does not have to mean “activate every operation and average them.” It can mean optimizing a differentiable distribution over discrete architectures.
