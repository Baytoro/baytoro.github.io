Neural Architecture Optimization

H.J. Chen
  2020-07-16 2020-07-16 Created  
  • Paper Reading 
 
  • NAS 

Luo R, Tian F, Qin T, et al. Neural Architecture Optimization. NeurIPS 2018.

Paper

NAO frames NAS as representation learning plus continuous optimization. Instead of searching directly in a discrete architecture space, it trains an encoder, a performance predictor, and a decoder. Architectures are mapped into a continuous latent space, moved toward higher predicted performance, then decoded back into discrete architectures.

This is an interesting alternative to RL, evolution, and differentiable supernets. It tries to make discrete architecture search look like gradient-based optimization in a learned space.

Encoder-Predictor-Decoder

The pipeline is:

  1. encode architecture (x) into latent vector (e_x);
  2. predict performance (f(e_x));
  3. update the latent vector by ascending predictor gradient;
  4. decode the improved vector into a new architecture.

Why This Is Useful

NAO separates architecture representation from architecture evaluation. If the encoder learns useful structural semantics, nearby points in latent space should decode to similar architectures with similar performance.

That gives the predictor gradient a chance to be meaningful. If the latent space is not smooth, the update becomes an artifact of the predictor.

Failure Modes

There are three major risks:

  • the predictor may rank architectures poorly;
  • the decoder may produce invalid or low-quality architectures after latent updates;
  • the latent step may leave the training distribution.

For this reason, predictor ranking correlation and decoder reconstruction accuracy are more informative than a single final accuracy number.

Relation To Later Work

NAO is connected to predictor-based NAS and generative architecture search. The general idea is still useful: learn an architecture embedding, then search in that embedding space.

Takeaway

NAO is not the final form of deployment NAS, but it is conceptually valuable. It shows that architecture search can be treated as continuous optimization if the architecture representation is learned carefully enough.
