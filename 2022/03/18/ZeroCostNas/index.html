Zero-Cost Proxies for Lightweight NAS

H.J. Chen
  2022-03-18 2022-03-18 Created  
  • Paper Reading 
 
  • NAS 

Abdelfattah M S, Mehrotra A, Dudziak L, Lane N D. Zero-Cost Proxies for Lightweight NAS.

Paper

Zero-cost NAS asks whether an architecture can be scored without training. Instead of training each candidate, it computes cheap signals at initialization, often using gradients, saliency, or Jacobian statistics.

The goal is not to perfectly replace training. The practical goal is to cheaply filter bad candidates or provide an early ranking signal.

Proxy Families

Several proxy types are commonly discussed:

SNIP

SNIP measures sensitivity of the loss to pruning weights:

The intuition is that important weights have high loss sensitivity even at initialization.

GraSP

GraSP focuses on preserving gradient flow after pruning. It uses second-order gradient information to estimate how removing weights affects training dynamics.

SynFlow

SynFlow is data-free. It propagates a synthetic signal through the network to measure path saliency and avoid layer collapse.

The figure compares zero-cost proxies on NAS-Bench-201. The useful question is not whether a proxy is perfect, but whether it is correlated enough to guide early search.

Failure Modes

Zero-cost scores are sensitive to:

  • initialization;
  • batch size;
  • normalization layers;
  • skip-connection density;
  • model size;
  • degenerate operations such as zero or identity.

A proxy may prefer larger models or architectures with better gradient flow even when they are not the best final trained models.

How I Would Use It

I would not select the final architecture using only a zero-cost proxy. I would use it as a first-stage filter:

  1. score many architectures cheaply;
  2. discard clearly bad candidates;
  3. train or surrogate-evaluate the remaining set.

Takeaway

Zero-cost proxies are useful diagnostics and filters. They are not a universal replacement for training, but they make architecture search much cheaper when used with calibration and validation.
