AutoFormer: Searching Transformers for Visual Recognition

H.J. Chen
  2022-09-19 2022-09-19 Created  
  • Work hard

  • PaperReading 
 
  • NAS 
  • | ViT

Chen M, Peng H, Fu J, Ling H. AutoFormer: Searching Transformers for Visual Recognition. ICCV 2021.

Paper / Code

AutoFormer is a one-shot NAS paper for pure Vision Transformers. The interesting part is not only that it searches depth, width, heads, and MLP ratios. The stronger claim is that a trained Transformer supernet can produce many subnets whose inherited weights are already close to retraining from scratch.

That is a high bar for one-shot NAS. If inherited accuracy is a poor proxy, the search loop is mostly ranking noise. AutoFormer tries to make the proxy reliable with weight entanglement: candidate Transformer blocks in the same layer are not trained as isolated choices, but share their common parameters in a nested way.

Motivation

For ViT, architecture choices are coupled. Increasing one dimension is not always better, and two models with similar parameter counts can behave very differently.

The sensitivity plot is a useful starting point. In this baseline experiment:

  • Depth improves accuracy until around 14 to 15 layers, then drops.
  • Embedding dimension gives a large jump early, then saturates.
  • MLP ratio peaks around 5 in this setting.
  • Head count is not monotonic when the Q-K-V dimension is fixed.

So the search space is not just “scale the model bigger”. AutoFormer searches a structured set of Transformer choices under parameter constraints.

Contribution

The paper contributes three things:

  1. It defines a ViT search space over embedding dimension, Q-K-V dimension, MLP ratio, number of heads, and depth.
  2. It proposes weight entanglement for one-shot Transformer supernet training.
  3. It searches tiny/small/base AutoFormers and shows that inherited-weight evaluation is close to fine-tuning or full retraining.

The third point is the most important one. If we only look at the final ImageNet table, this is another NAS result. If we look at the inherited-vs-retrained tables, the paper is really arguing that the supernet itself becomes a usable model family.

Search Space

AutoFormer builds three independent supernets:

Supernet Parameter range Intended scale
tiny 4-9M mobile/small models
small 14-34M DeiT-S/ViT-S scale
base 42-75M medium/base models

The table notation is (low, high, step). For example, Supernet-small searches embedding dimension from 320 to 448 with step 64, Q-K-V dimension from 320 to 448 with step 64, MLP ratio from 3 to 4 with step 0.5, head number from 5 to 7, and depth from 12 to 14.

The paper reports that the combined space contains more than candidate architectures. That explains why the method needs both weight sharing and a search algorithm; enumeration is not an option.

Weight Entanglement

Classical one-shot NAS often assigns separate weights to different choices in the same layer. AutoFormer argues that this is inefficient for Transformers because the candidate blocks are homogeneous: they are all attention/MLP blocks with different dimensions, not completely different operations like conv/pooling/skip in a DARTS cell.

Weight entanglement makes the smaller block use a subset of the larger block’s weights. For two candidate blocks in the same layer, their weights satisfy a nested relation:

In implementation terms, each layer only needs to store the largest candidate block. Smaller candidates directly slice the corresponding weights. A gradient update from one sampled subnet then trains weights that are also reused by other compatible subnets.

The training curve shows why this matters. Compared with classical weight sharing, weight entanglement converges faster and gives much higher inherited subnet accuracy during supernet training.

Search Pipeline

The pipeline has two phases:

  1. Train the supernet with weight entanglement by uniformly sampling subnets and updating their selected weights.
  2. Run evolutionary search under resource constraints on the trained supernet.

The supernet training recipe is close to DeiT: 500 epochs, AdamW, batch size 1024, cosine LR schedule, weight decay , 20 warmup epochs, label smoothing 0.1, drop path 0.1, RandAugment, CutMix, Mixup, and random erasing. The paper uses 16x16 patches.

For evolution search, AutoFormer reserves 10,000 ImageNet training examples as a validation set, starts from 50 random architectures, runs 20 generations, keeps the top 10 as parents, and uses crossover plus mutation. Depth mutation probability is 0.2 and block mutation probability is 0.4.

Experiments

Does Entanglement Fix The Proxy?

This is the key ablation.

The inherited-vs-retrained gap is the part to watch:

Method Inherited Retrain Observation
Classical sharing + random search 69.7% 80.1% inherited score is badly undertrained
Weight entanglement + random search 81.3% 81.4% inherited score is already useful
Classical sharing + evolution search 71.5% 80.4% evolution cannot fix a poor supernet proxy
Weight entanglement + evolution search 81.7% 81.7% search and retrain agree

This table is more convincing than the final top-1 table. It shows that the method improves the quality of the one-shot evaluator, not just the final selected architecture.

Evolution search also matters. The red candidates are consistently above the random baseline during search, which suggests the validation signal from inherited weights is informative enough for selection.

For the final AutoFormer-T/S/B models, inherited accuracy is nearly identical to fine-tuning or retraining:

Model Inherited Fine-tune Retrain
AutoFormer-T 74.7% 74.9% 74.9%
AutoFormer-S 81.7% 81.8% 81.7%
AutoFormer-B 82.4% 82.6% 82.6%

That is the core empirical claim: after weight-entangled training, the supernet is not just a search scaffold. It can directly provide deployable subnets.

The scatter plot makes the same point from another angle. Many high-performing subnets sampled from Supernet-small already land around 80.1% to 82.0% top-1 with inherited weights.

ImageNet Results

The front-page comparison plots top-1 accuracy against parameter count. It is not a full fairness proof because the models have different training recipes and inductive biases, but it gives a quick picture of where the searched AutoFormers sit among DeiT, ResNet, ResNeXt, and DenseNet references.

The compact comparison against manual Transformer baselines:

Model Params FLOPs Top-1
DeiT-tiny 5.7M 1.2G 72.2%
AutoFormer-tiny 5.7M 1.3G 74.7%
DeiT-S 22.1M 4.7G 79.9%
ViT-S/16 22.1M 4.7G 78.8%
AutoFormer-small 22.9M 5.1G 81.7%
DeiT-B 86M 18G 81.8%
AutoFormer-base 54M 11G 82.4%

AutoFormer-small is the cleanest result: roughly the same scale as DeiT-S and ViT-S/16, but +1.8 and +2.9 percentage points top-1, respectively. AutoFormer-base is also interesting because it beats DeiT-B while using fewer parameters and FLOPs.

The table also reminds me not to over-read the result as “Transformers are always more efficient”. EfficientNet-B4 and EfficientNet-B7 are still very strong in this comparison. AutoFormer is competitive among pure Transformers, but CNN/mobile design priors were still hard to beat in this 2021 setup.

Transfer And Distillation

AutoFormer-S fine-tuned at 384x384 reaches 83.4% ImageNet top-1 with 23M parameters and 16.5G FLOPs. In the same table, DeiT-B at 384x384 uses 86M parameters and 55.4G FLOPs for 83.1% ImageNet top-1.

The transfer table is useful because AutoFormer is not only a searched ImageNet classifier. It keeps competitive numbers on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Flowers, Cars, and Pets under the same fine-tuning style.

The paper also reports that knowledge distillation from a RegNetY-32GF teacher improves AutoFormer-T/S/B from 74.7%/81.7%/82.4% to 75.7%/82.4%/82.9%. That means the searched architecture is orthogonal to distillation; the search method is not relying on a teacher to make the main table work.

Relation To Other NAS Work

AutoFormer is close in spirit to Once-for-All and BigNAS: train one elastic supernet, then select subnets for different constraints. The difference is that AutoFormer is aimed at pure Transformer blocks. CNN elastic networks vary depth, width, kernel size, and resolution; AutoFormer varies Transformer-specific dimensions such as Q-K-V dimension, heads, and MLP ratio.

It is also worth comparing with ViTAS. Both papers care about whether weight sharing is fair enough for Transformer NAS. AutoFormer uses weight entanglement so that compatible candidates train shared slices of the same larger block. ViTAS later emphasizes channel fairness with cyclic sharing. They are attacking the same practical failure mode: inherited subnet ranking can be biased by uneven training.

Limitations

The method is not a free architecture search button.

  • The search space is still manually designed around a DeiT-style pure ViT. It does not search window attention, hierarchical stages, convolutional stems, or relative position designs.
  • The supernet training is expensive: 500 ImageNet epochs on V100 GPUs is not a small setup.
  • The inherited-weight claim depends on the weight-entangled training recipe. Classical weight sharing in the paper fails badly, so the result should not be generalized to arbitrary supernets.
  • The search objective is mainly ImageNet accuracy under parameter constraints. Real deployment may care more about latency, memory bandwidth, activation size, or hardware-specific kernels.

Takeaway

AutoFormer is useful because it makes one-shot Transformer NAS look credible. The searched models are good, but the more reusable lesson is the supernet design: when candidate Transformer blocks are nested and homogeneous, sharing the common weights can make inherited accuracy close to retrained accuracy.

For me, the paper’s strongest evidence is Table 2 and Table 4. They show that weight entanglement turns the supernet from a weak ranking proxy into something close to a deployable model family. That is the part worth remembering when reading later ViT NAS papers.
